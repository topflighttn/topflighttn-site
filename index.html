Safety & training

We take safety and training seriously

This is a club of pilots who keep training. Nearly every member is working toward an advanced rating, and more than half already hold a commercial certificate or higher. Our standards for flying the airplanes are set high on purpose.

High standards to fly solo

An instrument rating plus total-time minimums for each aircraft. Until you meet them, you fly with an approved instructor.

Cirrus-specific training

Cirrus ground school and transition training, then an instructor checkout in each airplane. Nobody flies an aircraft they have not been signed off in.

Equipment that adds margin

The CAPS parachute on every aircraft, known-ice protection on both SR22s, glass cockpits with autopilot, ADS-B traffic and weather, and hangar storage.

A culture of proficiency

Members fly together, share what they learn, and push each other toward the next rating.

Your path into the fleet

From new member to flying solo in a Cirrus

Membership is open to current pilots and to people working toward their certificates. Every member follows the same path, and you can earn your instrument rating in club aircraft.

  1. Cirrus ground school

    Systems, CAPS deployment scenarios, and Perspective avionics before you touch the airplane.

  2. Cirrus transition training

    Flight training with a Cirrus-qualified instructor covering normal, abnormal and emergency procedures.

  3. Aircraft checkout

    An instructor signs you off in each airplane separately. Perspective and Perspective+ are different enough to warrant it.

  4. Instrument rating

    Required to fly the club Cirrus solo. You can train for it in club aircraft with an instructor on board.

  5. Solo in the fleet

    Once you meet the hours minimum for each aircraft, book it and go.

Every reservation lives in Flight Circle. Booking limits keep any one member from tying up an airplane, so weekend and trip availability stays real for everyone. Limits apply per member, per aircraft. Full rules are in the member bylaws.

Questions

Before you ask

Do I need to be a pilot to join?

No. Membership is open to pilots and to people working toward their certificates. You fly club aircraft with an instructor until you meet the insurance requirements for solo.

Why do I need an instrument rating to fly solo?

Our insurer requires it for solo operation of the club Cirrus aircraft, along with minimum total time (100+ hours for the SR20, 200+ for the SR22s). You can train for the rating in club aircraft.

What does "dry rate" mean?

The hourly rate excludes fuel. You buy fuel for your own flights. Dry rates are set to cover engine reserves and variable costs and may be adjusted by the board.

How hard is it to get an airplane?

With 21 members across 3 aircraft, and booking limits of one 7-day trip and one 36-hour reservation per member per aircraft, weekends and trips stay realistic for everyone. Being checked out in all three aircraft gives you the most flexibility.

What happens to my equity if I leave?

Equity exit and transfer terms are set in the club bylaws, which you review before joining.

Can the costs go up?

Yes. Dues, hourly rates and capital calls are set by the board based on actual club costs: insurance, hangar, maintenance, financing and reserves. Every member shares them equally.
